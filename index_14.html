⏰ ~15 min 🏠 No equipment
For 15U athletes · use 2–3x/week before practice, for 3–4 weeks. Goal: elasticity → reactive power → max jump.
1. Pogo Warm-Up — 4 min
  • 2-Foot Pogos2 x 15
  • 1-Foot Pogos2 x 8/leg
  • Lateral Pogos2 x 10 each dir.
  • Front/Back Pogos2 x 10
Focus: stiff ankles, short ground contact, rhythmic bouncing.
2. Reactive Jumps — 5 min
  • Snap Down → Immediate Vertical Jump2 x 4
  • Lateral Bound → Quick Vertical Jump2 x 3/side
  • Repeated Vertical Jumps2 x 4
Focus: hit the ground and get back up fast. Avoid sinking into a deep squat.
3. Max-Effort Jumps — 5–6 min
  • Countermovement Vertical Jump3 x 2
  • Approach Jump (dunk/rebound takeoff)3 x 2
  • Standing Broad Jump2 x 2
Rest 30–45 sec between sets. Full reset between reps. Every jump is max intent.
⚡ Key rule: high quality, low fatigue — stop when jump quality drops. Pogos = quick · Reactive = fast off the ground · Max Jumps = as high/far as possible. Finish feeling explosive, not fatigued.

Quick, practical basics for 15–16 year old players — and parents — to build into the daily routine. Not medical advice; check with a doctor for individual concerns.
🍏 Nutrition
Fuel for growth, practice & recovery
  • Eat every 3–4 hours — never skip breakfast, it sets up the whole day.
  • Protein at every meal: eggs, chicken, greek yogurt, beans. Growing & recovering muscles need it.
  • Carbs are fuel, not the enemy — rice, oats, fruit, whole-grain bread before & after training.
  • Pre-practice (60–90 min before): banana + peanut butter, or a granola bar.
  • Post-practice (within 30–60 min): protein + carbs — chocolate milk, turkey sandwich, yogurt + fruit.
  • Water all day, every day — aim for pale yellow. Refill the bottle at school and before practice.
  • Go easy on soda & energy drinks — they hurt sleep and don't actually fuel performance.
😴 Sleep
The #1 recovery tool — free and non-negotiable
  • Target 8–10 hours a night — growing teen athletes need more than adults, not less.
  • Same bed time and wake time daily, weekends included — consistency matters more than one long sleep-in.
  • Phones/screens off 30–60 min before bed — the light delays your body's sleep signal.
  • Keep the room dark, cool, and quiet. Charge phones outside the bedroom if possible.
  • No caffeine after 3–4 PM (includes energy drinks and most pop).
  • Short 20–30 min naps are fine if tired — avoid long or late-afternoon naps that wreck nighttime sleep.
📅 Time Management
Balancing school, practice & rest
  • Use a weekly planner (paper or phone) — block out school, practice, homework, and bedtime.
  • Start homework right after school when possible — energy and focus fade later in the evening.
  • Pack the gym bag & prep gear the night before practice — one less rushed decision in the morning.
  • Break big assignments into smaller chunks across the week instead of cramming the night before.
  • Flag conflicts early — tell teachers about game/tournament dates as soon as they're known.
  • Protect sleep first — it's never worth trading for extra late-night screen time or cramming.
